# Office Status Slider

A 3D-printable office status sign. Status labels sit along a dovetail track on the plate, and a frame slides along the track to show your current status. It can also carry a name, printed on the plate or on a slide-in tag, or be a name sign on its own.

The page lets you design the sign in your browser and download print-ready STL files.

## What you can change

- **Statuses:** 2–12 labels with your own text and 36 icons (meeting, call, focus, lab, lunch, remote, travel, business trip, client visit, factory, stepped out, vacation, sick, editing, color grading, rendering, reviewing, deadline, idea, writing, presenting, six mood faces and more), or pick from the ready-made statuses (English). **Presets** are whole designs: each sets its statuses and a look to go with them (shape, typeface, layout and colors), while your name, mounting, size and printer settings stay. Among them: office, hybrid, studio, lab, classroom, clinic and home sets, **Mood** (great to awful), and in Traditional Chinese a general office set, **剪輯師** (on the film strip; video editor: 剪輯中, 調色中, 算圖中, 審片中, 趕片中), **企劃** (on the clipboard; planner: 發想中, 寫腳本, 開會中, 提案中, 勘景中) and **心情** (超開心 to 崩潰), plus Japanese.
- **Shape:** 18 designs. **Rail only** is the default (just the rail, frame and the smallest back panel). Others: rounded, pill, **Classic** (English door-plate style with a raised double bead and serif lettering), **Honeycomb** (hexagon-perforated border, holes all the way round), arrow, lozenge, banner, pixel, cat ears, ticket, cloud, speech bubble, scallop, wave, arch, **Film strip** (sprocket holes through the plate along the top and bottom) and **Clipboard** (a raised clip with rivets and a hanging slot at the top).
- **Rails:** on Rail only, Classic, Honeycomb, Puzzle, Folder and Wave the rails stop at the ends of the track, inside the border or end features. On every other shape they run out to the edge of the plate and follow its outline, with the left end filled solid; the side margin keeps them back from the edge if you want a border. An optional bevel rounds off the outer edge of the whole base.
- **Size:** by default the plate fits itself to your text at the letter height you choose. You can also set width and height by hand. Either way it's checked against your printer's bed size.
- **Printer rules:** set the nozzle and layer height at the top. Every height the printer builds layer by layer (plate, rails, lettering, tag, bevel steps, magnet pockets, the frame) is a whole number of layers, walls are at least two nozzle widths, and letters, frame bars and clearances have nozzle-based minimums, whether you drag a slider or type a value. A few settings go below zero: lean the stand forward, sink the frame below the rail tops (it never drops into its own dovetail), or hang the sign higher than the cubicle panel or rail slot. The name margin and the status text padding may go below zero too, to set the letters closer to the edges. If a change can't be built, a message explains why for a few seconds and the setting goes back to the last value that worked. Turn on Dynamic to drop the layer rule for adaptive layer heights.
- **Tabs:** Shape (outline and how it mounts), Plate (bevel, size, margins, thickness), Status (the status track, label layout, lettering, the sliding frame and the track) and Name. The status track can be turned off for a sign with only a name.
- **Lettering:** typeface (13 to choose from), raised or engraved. The status labels and the name each have their own thickness.
- **3D view:** a view cube next to the view buttons turns with the model; click a face for a straight-on (orthographic) view, and Reset view goes back to 3D.
- **Colors:** the palette button on the 3D view sets every preview color in one place (plate, status lettering, frame, name text, name tag) or applies a ready-made palette. The colors carry into the 3MF. Text prints exactly as typed and supports Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, Vietnamese, Traditional Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Hebrew and Thai. Arabic and Indic scripts need text shaping and are not supported.
- **Mounting:** flat back (optionally with a recess for a tape pad), magnet pockets (size, count and distance from the ends), screw holes (hole and head diameter), desk-stand feet (lean, width, and separate forward and backward extension), a **hang loop** (one or two eyelets above any shape), a **cubicle hook** (two printed hooks that clip onto the sign and hang over the top of a cubicle panel, like an upside-down J; set the panel thickness) Both cubicle hooks set the gap between hook and sign inside the clip, and how far two small ridges inside the clip bite into the sign on each face (a deeper bite makes a wider contact strip and a tighter hold; 0 leaves them off), so the hook does not wobble. Or a **cubicle rail** hanger for a C-shaped rail: each hook ends in a thin tab that goes through the rail's slot while you hold the sign up a little, then drops behind the lower lip and rests on the floor of the hollow behind it. It slides along the rail. Set the slot height (10 mm), the lip depth (1 mm), how far the hollow reaches past the slot (2 mm up and down) and its depth (1 mm). Shape and mounting work together: changing either one changes the final model.
- **Name:** on the Name tab, off, printed on the plate, or on a **slide-in tag**: a separate flat tag in a 45° dovetail rail above the track, like the status track, so it prints without supports. The rail between the tag and the status track is shared, which keeps the sign shorter; the track's rail height and the tag's thickness still set independently, and the tag has its own slide clearance. The tag rail height sets how high the rails' 45° holder rises. It is never lower than the tag; at the tag's thickness the holder just meets the tag's bevelled edges, and above it the holder keeps leaning in over the edges of the tag's top face (with a small gap) so the tag can't fall out. The band grows to match, so the name keeps its size. On the pixel, cloud, scallop, wave and arch shapes, and on every shape but Classic and Honeycomb when the sign hangs from cubicle hooks or a cubicle rail, the tag's upper rail runs out to the outline above it, so the hooks clip over the full thickness of plate and rail. With a hang loop on those shapes, the rings rise to the rail tops too. On the arch, where the tag sits in the crown, the strip between the tag and the status track is filled to the same height, so both rails share one flat top. On the scallop, wave and speech shapes the status track's lower rail runs out to the outline below it (the speech bubble's tail included), and on the wave every rail reaches every edge: the sides, and the top as well when no name sits above the track. Desk-stand feet clip over the full thickness there, like the cubicle hooks. A name margin sets the space around the name. The tag rail is as long as the main rails; on the shapes whose rails run to the edge, the tag follows the outline at both ends, so it is symmetric whenever the plate is, and the stop at its end leans over the tag's bevelled end the same way, so the border is even all round, round corners included. It slides in from the right end, like the frame, and stops at the left end; drag it in the preview to see it move. Change the name, download again and print a new tag for the next person; the sign itself stays the same.

## Printing

**Download** opens a dialog with a ZIP of every part, already oriented for printing with no supports needed, plus a README with print and assembly notes. The same dialog offers a single **3MF** file with the parts laid out on your bed and the preview colors, for Bambu Studio, Orca Slicer and PrusaSlicer.

- For a multi-color printer (AMS, MMU), load `base_body` and `base_labels` together as one object.
- For a single nozzle, the page shows which layers to swap filament at.

To assemble, slide the frame into the track from the right end, the open end. There is no stop pin: the frame is held only by the fit of the dovetail, so don't push it past the last status. The name tag, if you made one, slides into its rail from the right end too.

## Notes

- The whole tool is the single file `index.html`. It works on phones too: the preview stays at the top of the screen while you scroll through the settings.
- It loads three.js, Manifold and the label fonts from jsDelivr, so it needs an internet connection.
- **Copy design link** saves your settings in the URL, so you can reopen or share an exact design.
